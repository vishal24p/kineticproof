# First scientific investigation — at-home pendulum (not entire product)

**Question:** Can a student obtain a trustworthy oscillation period from an ordinary phone video, and how does visual clutter affect the reliability of different tracking methods?

**Materials:** thin non-stretch thread; small secure padlock or metal nut/washer (doesn't have to be a ball); sturdy fixed overhead anchor/rod; ruler or measuring tape; phone with fixed support; simple high-contrast background for control. No formal lab required.

**Setup:** Tie thread securely to stationary support and object. Ensure free movement and no impact with people/glass; keep phone stationary facing the swing plane; capture entire arc, pivot and enough cycles. Measure length from pivot to **suspended object's center of mass**, not just thread length. Start at modest angle (~5–10°), release without a push.

**Three controlled conditions:** (A) clear background; (B) cluttered static background; (C) clutter plus moving background/brief occlusion. Try to hold length, camera perspective, starting angle and lighting fixed; log deviations. Record ~20–30 sec per take when feasible, with enough visible cycles. Repetition and eventual multiple lengths are more useful than theatrical clutter alone.

**Independent reference:** Manually time several whole cycles from the original video, or obtain independently recorded timing; annotate actual timestamps (no inferred from AI). This reference is separate from theoretical period. Respect rights/consent of anyone visible or avoid filming identifiable bystanders.

**Not yet performed:** These instructions are for future recordings, not evidence that any footage was collected.
